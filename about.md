## Bryan Collick

Bryan Collick built operational governance at Braze through and after IPO: a tech vendor governance framework from scratch, governance over both capitalized R&D (~$5M annually) and the federal R&D tax credit (~$6M annually) under clean-audit controls, M&A integration of operational functions, and Looker-based reporting that the board, finance, and operating teams use. Earlier, he coordinated regulatory examination response at LendUp during active state and federal regulatory examinations, and self-certification work at Babylon Health. The through-line across these is not the regulation in each case; it's the underlying operational layer that had to be designed for any of them to produce defensible outcomes. South Pillar is the practice he founded to do that work for other companies, through contracted engagement rather than as full-time staff.

The practice exists because the work has a shape. The operational layer underneath audit, compliance, finance, and risk fails in patterned ways at growth-stage companies: policies inherited from templates, controls accreted from audit findings, processes that diverge from what's written, continuous review that quietly disappears the first time a quarter goes sideways. The patterns are visible from the inside, and they are fixable — but not by adding tooling or headcount on top of a broken chain. The method South Pillar uses is called Goal-Anchored Operations, and it is documented in full at [methodology.md](./methodology.md).

South Pillar is based in Texas. Engagements are remote-first, with on-site working sessions where the work calls for them.
