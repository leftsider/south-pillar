Engagements

South Pillar offers five engagement formats. Each service starts with The Briefing. The right format for a given company depends on where the chain is weakest today — which is exactly what The Briefing reveals and documents.

01

The Briefing

A 60- to 90-minute working session with the executive responsible for the function. The output is a written read, delivered within five business days, naming the two or three links in the chain that are weakest in your operation today and what a credible next step looks like. The Briefing is paid. Most companies that take The Briefing do not necessarily move directly to a larger engagement. The ones that do, do so because the read made the case.

For: CFOs, Controllers, COOs, GCs, CCOs, CISOs, Chief of Staff equivalents, and founders at growth- and expansion-stage companies (typically Series B–D) who suspect the operational layer underneath audit, compliance, or reporting is paying for itself twice, and want a practitioner's read before committing to a larger engagement.

Not for: Anyone looking for a free 30-minute discovery call. The Briefing is the work, not a prelude to the work.

02

The Diagnostic

A four– to six-week scoped assessment of one operational domain, usually anchored to an externally-driven event. The Diagnostic maps the five-link chain inside that domain end-to-end: goals at the top, continuous review at the bottom, and every policy, control, and process in between. The output is a 15 to 25 page written assessment that names every detached link, every control without a policy anchor, every policy without a control, and the reactive tax the company is paying as a result. Companies use the diagnostic to decide whether to commission the design engagement, to staff the work internally, or to accept the risk and do nothing — all three are legitimate outcomes.

For: Companies with an externally-anchored event on the calendar — ideally 90 to 180 days out, though earlier is a mark of maturity — senior compliance, legal, security, or finance leadership in seat, and a sponsor at the executive level.

Not for: Companies that already know what they want built and are looking for additional hands. Go straight to The Design Engagement.

03

The Design Engagement

Ten to fourteen weeks of design work, developed with the internal owners of the operational layer, to produce a design proposal for closing the gaps The Diagnostic surfaced. The output is a design that is feasible, natural, and persistent — grounded in the specifics of the business, defensible under review, and structured so the chain holds together after the practitioner leaves. The Design Engagement is not a document exercise. It is a working design the client is ready to implement.

For: Companies that have taken The Diagnostic or have an equivalent internal read, with an executive sponsor and a named internal counterpart who will own the chain after the engagement ends.

Not for: Companies that want the practitioner to do the work alone and hand it over. Goal-Anchored Operations does not survive that handoff.

04

The Steady State

A monthly retainer for companies that have completed The Design Engagement and want the practitioner to continue running the continuous review link. The work is bounded: read the prior month's evidence, name what changed, decide whether anything upstream needs to be rewritten, and put the answer in writing. Engagement is month-to-month, and either side can end this service with thirty days' notice.

For: Companies one to twelve months out from a completed Design Engagement, where the internal counterpart is in seat but the continuous review cadence is still hardening.

Not for: Companies that want a fractional Head of Compliance. The Steady State is the fifth link of the chain, not a seat at the table.

05

The Build

Not currently offered.

The Build is where South Pillar works alongside the internal team to implement the design produced in The Design Engagement — standing up controls, wiring processes into daily operating cadence, instrumenting the evidence layer, and running the first continuous-review cycles in seat. The output is the design in production, running under the client's ownership.

For: Companies that have completed The Design Engagement and want practitioner capacity in seat during implementation.

Not for: Companies looking to outsource ownership of the operational layer.

Design notes & Google Sites tradeoffs

What you're looking at

This is the v3.1 tone pass on top of the merged v3 revision. Portfolio unchanged (five formats). Micro-edits: hyphenation on "60- to 90-minute" and "four– to six-week"; Briefing text softens "do not move directly" to "do not necessarily move directly"; Diagnostic gains "usually anchored to…" and "accept the risk and do nothing"; Diagnostic fit window narrows from 60–180 days to 90–180 days (consistent with the 60-day floor now being a disqualifier for engagements broadly).

This is a faithful static mockup of the approved design system. Type sizes, spacing, colors, and the five-link chain are exact. Use it to judge the aesthetic; the live build will happen in Google Sites and may diverge in the ways noted below.

Placeholders: bryan@southpillarllc.com and the github.com/southpillar/south-pillar canonical link in the footer are both placeholders pending final domain and email decisions.

Google Sites parity check

  • FULL Custom hex palette (5 colors) — Google Sites custom theme accepts any hex.
  • FULL Source Serif 4 + Inter — both available via "More fonts" in the theme editor.
  • FULL Five-link chain — built as an embedded HTML/CSS block; identical to this preview.
  • FULL 680px prose width — achieved by leaving outer grid columns empty.
  • PARTIAL Bronze underlines on inline links — Sites supports custom link colors but with less precise control over offset/thickness. Expect ~80% fidelity.
  • PARTIAL Eyebrow letter-spacing — Sites doesn't expose letter-spacing directly; close using its "small heading" style.
  • PARTIAL 64px bronze rule — Sites' divider can't be resized to exactly 64px; we'll use a 64×1 PNG colored bronze. Visually identical.
  • NOT IN SITES Sticky header with subtle scroll behavior — Sites uses a fixed nav style. Acceptable compromise.
  • NOT IN SITES This annotation drawer — mockup-only.

Page-specific notes

  • Engagements are numbered 01–05 in eyebrows. The number signals "there are exactly five" rather than decoration.
  • "For / Not for" italic blocks do the qualification work that pricing usually does on competitor sites.
  • No prices anywhere on the page. The Briefing is the qualification gate.

What's intentionally absent

  • No buttons. Inline text links only.
  • No icons, no decorative graphics, no stock imagery.
  • No social proof, no testimonials, no client logos.
  • No pricing.
  • No form. Contact is mailto only.
  • No named regulations or standards. The methodology is the product.
